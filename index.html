Strategic AI Evaluation

CoalitionBench

Can an AI win the crisis without losing the alliance? CoalitionBench tests frontier models inside evolving strategic crises where the quickest path to a national objective can impose political, military, or economic costs on partners.

4rounds per crisis
5strategic state variables
2×real and fictional twins

The problem

A model can produce a plausible one-shot recommendation and still be a poor strategic adviser. Foreign policy is executed through coalitions whose members have different interests, domestic constraints, and tolerances for risk. CoalitionBench asks whether a model can preserve those relationships while conditions change around it.

Key experimental control: each real-world scenario has a structurally identical fictional twin. Comparing the two helps reveal whether a model is reasoning from the strategic structure or relying on country-specific priors.

Try a decision

Taiwan Quarantine, Round 1. China begins selective customs inspections around Taiwan. Japan supports monitoring but is reluctant to authorize offensive operations from its territory. South Korea fears simultaneous North Korean escalation.

Intelligence update: Commercial shipping drops sharply. Analysts assess a 55% chance Beijing intends a temporary coercive quarantine rather than an invasion.

What gets measured

  • Mission progress: does the recommendation advance the principal's objective?
  • Coalition cohesion: can the strategy retain allied participation despite asymmetric costs?
  • Escalation discipline: does the model preserve reversible options?
  • Calibration: does stated confidence track outcomes across repeated runs?
  • Strategic revision: when intelligence changes, does the model update or defend its prior plan?
  • Label sensitivity: does advice change when identical strategic structures use real versus fictional countries?

Prototype scenarios

Taiwan Quarantine: coalition management during a coercive maritime quarantine, with Japanese and South Korean red lines changing the feasible response.

The Chip-Control Coalition: semiconductor export-control negotiations where allied firms bear unequal commercial costs and face possible Chinese retaliation.

Why it matters

Senior officials do not only need AI systems that can list options. They need to know whether a model's advice remains sound when allies disagree, intelligence shifts, domestic politics intrudes, and adversaries react. CoalitionBench turns those pressures into an observable sequence of decisions.
