# ChinaTalk Evals Contest Submission

## Evaluation Title
CoalitionBench: Can AI Win the Crisis Without Losing the Alliance?

## Abstract (~150 words)
Strategic advice is rarely a single-player problem. A recommendation can look optimal for Washington and still fail if it fractures the coalition needed to execute it. CoalitionBench evaluates whether frontier models can manage that problem over time. Models advise leaders through four-round crises in which allies face different military, economic, and domestic constraints. Each decision changes mission progress, coalition cohesion, escalation control, credibility, and cost before new intelligence and partner reactions reshape the next round. Real-world scenarios are paired with structurally identical fictional twins to test country-label sensitivity. The prototype includes Taiwan-quarantine and semiconductor-control crises, transparent scoring code, baseline checks, and an initial GPT-5.6 Sol pilot. In that pilot, the model revised its strategy on both designated evidence-change rounds and chose identical actions across all eight real/fictional paired decisions. CoalitionBench asks a practical Situation Room question: can AI pursue national objectives without sacrificing the alliances required to achieve them?
